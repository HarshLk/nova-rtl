# NOVA-RTL: Evidence-Grounded Generative AI for Formally Verified RTL Timing Closure

NOVA-RTL: Neuro-symbolic Optimization and Verification Architecture for RTL, is an end-to-end Generative-AI-assisted framework for accelerating timing closure in complex multi-clock digital designs. Rather than allowing an LLM to rewrite Verilog without control, NOVA-RTL combines AI reasoning with deterministic RTL transformations, formal verification, and authoritative EDA measurements.

The framework first ingests the RTL, SDC constraints, libraries, clock definitions, reset behavior, and protected CDC structures into an immutable Design Contract. Yosys, OpenSTA, and OpenROAD then generate synthesis, timing, structural, physical, clock, and CDC evidence. This information is normalized into a Design Evidence Graph, allowing the system to cluster critical paths, trace mapped logic back to RTL, and classify bottlenecks such as excessive logic depth, priority chains, high fanout, arithmetic complexity, FSM/PSM structure, reconvergence, routing pressure, and poor register placement.

A constrained AI planning layer converts these evidence-backed opportunities into typed optimization proposals. NOVA-RTL gives users the option to run the flow in 3 different modes: deterministic heuristics, a single constrained AI agent, or a bounded multi-agent council containing timing-forensics, logic, arithmetic, FSM, retiming, pipelining, fanout, formal-verification, and PPA specialists. Agents receive a small common safety contract and private role-specific evidence packs, reason independently in a blinded first round, and submit structured proposals to a coordinating chair. This reduces token consumption, protects RTL intellectual property, limits groupthink, and produces meaningful technical disagreement.

The AI never directly controls acceptance or unrestricted RTL generation. Approved proposals are materialized through a deterministic Transformation Registry supporting logic restructuring, Boolean simplification, arithmetic optimization, mux balancing, resource and fanout restructuring, retiming, FSM/PSM optimization, and explicitly permitted pipelining. Every modification creates an immutable child in a reversible Candidate DAG.

Candidates pass through progressively stronger gates: schema and policy validation, elaboration, protected clock/reset/CDC checks, synthesis, formal verification, multi-domain STA, and physical evaluation. EQY and SymbiYosys provide strict sequential equivalence, retiming-aware equivalence, or latency-aware transaction proofs depending on the transformation. OpenSTA and OpenROAD measure actual WNS, TNS, achievable frequency, area, power, congestion, and physical correlation. Only formally valid candidates enter a measured multi-objective Pareto frontier.

NOVA-RTL also includes a failure-aware recovery plane that translates tool failures into structured diagnostics, detects semantically repetitive candidates, applies bounded repair budgets, and decides whether to revise parameters, switch transformation families, reanalyse the opportunity, branch from another candidate, or stop. An append-only experiment ledger provides complete provenance, replayability, and learning without allowing failed attempts to silently repeat.

The proposed benchmark contains five asynchronous master clock domains, 21 generated clocks per master, multiple clock-divider ratios, realistic CDC structures, and approximately 50,000 standard cells. The interactive demonstration will allow judges to explore critical paths, inspect AI proposals and specialist critiques, observe unsafe candidates being rejected, follow recovery from a failed optimization, compare the Pareto candidates, and trace every reported improvement to its formal proof and raw EDA artifact.

NOVA-RTL does not ask engineers to trust AI-generated RTL. It lets AI propose intelligent experiments—and requires formal proof and silicon-relevant measurements to earn acceptance.
